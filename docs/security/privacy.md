# Client data and privacy

Client addresses, contact details, insurance identifiers, representation references, Pflegegrad records, and notes are personal data. They remain in the tenant-owned client module and are protected by API authorization, scoped parent-client access, application tenant predicates, and forced PostgreSQL RLS.

The application does not log client request bodies or child-resource values. Audit metadata records resource IDs and changed-field names rather than copies of personal values. Versioned outbox payloads use resource IDs and operation metadata. Client timeline responses are a client-oriented projection and omit raw audit metadata. Validation errors are generic and do not echo submitted values. Insurance identifiers are only rendered in the authorized client detail workflow and are never included in list search or timeline output.

All users with an applicable `client.read` grant can read the client child resources through the API; this slice does not define separate permissions for insurance identifiers or contact details. Review this access boundary before adding broader caregiver workflows. Contact notes are length-limited and the UI explicitly discourages medical information there.

Service catalog data is operational configuration. It is tenant-isolated and has separate `service.read` and catalog mutation permissions. It contains no prices, claims, or billing records.

Scheduling reveals that a client has a planned visit, along with only the display name, provider, time, planned service snapshots, status, and assigned employee names needed for coordination. Calendar responses omit insurance, Pflegegrad, contacts, and full client history. Visit notes are not copied into audit metadata or outbox events. Caregiver execution views contain only operational visit data and require an active employee link and assignment. Execution details follow visit/client scope for Office readers. Operational notes and service notes are not copied into audit metadata or outbox events; the client timeline contains only high-level started/completed summaries. A planned visit is not a care record or evidence of service delivery.

Care documentation entries, service-evidence notes, and amendments contain sensitive narrative care data. They are available only through authorized visit/documentation detail reads; the Office worklist returns metadata and structured missing-requirement codes without note snippets. There is no full-text search. Audit metadata, documentation history metadata, and outbox payloads contain identifiers and change metadata but never narrative text. The client timeline shows fixed summaries only for incident recording, finalization, and amendment. Finalized content remains immutable; corrections append a separately attributed amendment.

Structured clinical measurements, observations, assessments, and risks are also sensitive. Only authorized clinical detail and bounded history routes return their values or notes. Caregiver history is limited to assigned or participating visits; Office results are resource-scope filtered before projection. Audit and outbox records contain identifiers, record kinds, changed fields, and versions, never clinical values or notes. The clinical module adds no full-text search, broad worklist fields, automatic alerts, diagnoses, or timeline entries. Clinical values are not included in service-evidence packages or acknowledgement screens.

Care-planning records contain sensitive personal perspective and professional assessment. Authorized detail views may expose the plan's perspective, needs, resources, goals, measures, risk references, and evaluations. Worklists, audit metadata, outbox payloads, and broad timeline summaries must not copy this narrative content; they should carry only identifiers, lifecycle metadata, and changed-field names. Clinical records remain the source of clinical facts: a plan should reference relevant records deliberately rather than duplicate a client's clinical history. Care-plan narrative does not belong in service-evidence packages or acknowledgement screens.

Service-evidence packages contain only the service facts shown to a signatory and are immutable snapshots. Acknowledgement history snapshots the signatory's name and capacity. Decline notes are protected detail data and are omitted from audit, logs, and outbox events. Signature strokes are sensitive personal data stored in a private S3-compatible bucket; PostgreSQL holds an opaque key and SHA-256 digest. Authorized retrieval verifies the signature and package hashes and returns the stroke representation without a public or permanent object URL. Worklists expose no signature bytes. The current system retains signature artifacts with acknowledgement history and has no automatic deletion policy.

## Workforce qualifications and readiness

Employee qualification assignments, validity dates, revocation history, and operation-readiness findings are sensitive personnel data. Access requires dedicated Workforce Compliance read authority and is further limited to the employee scopes the reader may access. Scope filtering occurs before worklist pagination and before counts or aggregates are calculated. Detailed employee findings use the separate detail permission.

Compliance responses contain only the employee and qualification identifiers and display fields needed to act on the finding, its derived status and dates, affected operation, policy version, severity, and stable finding code. They do not expose certificate files, unrelated contact information, private personnel notes, or clinical narrative. Qualification readiness is an operational projection; it is not a licensing determination, regulatory certification, or conclusion that an employee is noncompliant when no responsibility mapping exists.

Dashboard reads and time-derived expirations do not create audit or outbox records. Changes to tenant thresholds or reporting timezone are audited and emit a versioned settings event containing the tenant, settings version, and changed fields, without employee qualification or contact values. The reporting timezone follows facility, organization, and tenant settings; it is independent of the user's interface language. This slice has no notification or renewal side effect.

## Medication records

Medication definitions, order revisions, administration occurrences, administration records, and correction history are sensitive health information. Office order and history routes require medication permission and client resource scope. Caregiver medication views require an active linked employee and assignment to the requested visit, and return only medication facts needed for that visit. Client history requires an explicitly bounded date range.

The medication tables force tenant RLS and use tenant-qualified relationships. Finalized order revisions, administration records, and corrections cannot be updated or deleted; a correction appends attributed history. Audit and outbox metadata contain resource identifiers, action, and version only, never medication names, doses, notes, or administration reasons. The local catalog is not a verified drug database, and the platform does not prescribe, dispense, evaluate interactions, or infer clinical suitability. See [Medication management and administration](../domains/medication.md).
